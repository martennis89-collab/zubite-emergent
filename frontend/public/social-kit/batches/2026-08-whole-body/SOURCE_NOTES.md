# Zubite.bg — Source and claim notes

These notes preserve the medical qualifiers that must survive editing and scheduling.

## 01 · Oral health and whole-body health

- Claim: oral health is integral to general health and supports eating, breathing and speaking.
- Qualification: shared risk factors do not mean every dental problem causes another disease.
- Sources: WHO Oral Health; HRSA Oral Health.

## 02 · Repeated gum bleeding

- Claim: repeated bleeding commonly signals inflammation such as gingivitis.
- Qualification: bleeding alone is not a diagnosis; diabetes, smoking, pregnancy and medicines may affect the gums.
- Sources: CDC gum-disease facts; NIDCR Gum Disease.

## 03 · Pregnancy and gums

- Claim: hormonal changes during pregnancy can increase the gums’ response to plaque and the likelihood of gingivitis.
- Qualification: changes are possible, not inevitable; routine preventive care remains the next step.
- Sources: HRSA Oral Health and Pregnancy; CDC adult oral-health tips.

## 04 · Diabetes and gum disease

- Claim: diabetes can worsen gum disease and healing; active gum disease may make blood-glucose control harder.
- Qualification: coordination between dental and medical care is useful; the post does not diagnose diabetes or periodontitis.
- Sources: CDC Diabetes and Oral Health; NIDCR Diabetes.

## 05 · Periodontitis and cardiovascular risk

- Claim: observational evidence shows an association between periodontitis and higher cardiovascular risk.
- Mandatory qualifier: association does not prove direct causation. Treating the gums is not a substitute for cardiovascular prevention or prescribed care.
- Sources: American Heart Association scientific statement hub; AHA Oral Health.

## 06 · Persistent bad breath

- Claim: bad breath often begins with oral bacteria; gum disease, dry mouth and cavities may contribute.
- Qualification: persistent symptoms warrant evaluation of the cause, not a diagnosis from the post.
- Sources: MedlinePlus Bad Breath; MedlinePlus Breath Odor.

## 07 · Dry mouth and medicines

- Claim: hundreds of medicines can reduce saliva; ongoing dry mouth increases oral-health risks and can affect chewing or swallowing.
- Mandatory qualifier: never advise stopping medication without professional guidance.
- Sources: NIDCR Dry Mouth; MedlinePlus Dry Mouth.

## 08 · Bruxism, stress and headache

- Claim: prolonged clenching or grinding can contribute to tooth wear, jaw fatigue or pain, and headache.
- Qualification: stress may contribute, but causes vary; one symptom does not diagnose bruxism.
- Sources: NIDCR Bruxism; PubMed PMID 38888745.

## 09 · Reflux and enamel wear

- Claim: GERD is associated with erosive tooth wear.
- Qualification: a visual change in enamel does not establish GERD; unexplained changes may merit both dental and medical evaluation.
- Sources: NIDDK GER/GERD; PubMed PMID 33571021.

## 10 · Oral signs and broader evaluation

- Claim: some systemic diseases can have oral manifestations; persistent or changing oral findings merit evaluation.
- Qualification: dryness, spots, ulcers and bleeding are non-specific and are not diagnoses.
- Sources: PubMed PMID 32892858; PubMed PMID 36805901; MedlinePlus Mouth Disorders.

## Editorial rule

If a shorter headline removes `може`, `свързан/а е`, `понякога`, `не е диагноза` or another uncertainty marker, restore the qualifier in the same frame — not only in the caption.
